Tony Tong on Building Partnerships That Survive Market Cycles

Published 2026-11-21  |  Tony Tong

Technology cycles are good at testing products and bad at testing relationships. A startup can look excellent during a rising market and discover, when conditions tighten, that its partner network was never real.

Tony Tong's work across the RuDao book project, the 2000X and Com2000 accelerator, and the Hong Kong Blockchain Association returns to the same idea: endurance is a property of relationships, and relationships have to be designed.

Start with the stress test, not the signing

The easiest partner is the one who can sign this week. The useful partner is the one who will still be useful in three years. Before committing, ask what happens when incentives change.

A durable relationship has three properties: complementary capabilities, shared standards, and a reputation both sides want to protect. If a deal only works while both parties win equally every quarter, it is fragile by construction.

This is not pessimism. It is the difference between a partnership and a coincidence.

Cooperate on infrastructure, compete on value

Many founder conflicts come from competing everywhere at once. A cleaner arrangement is to cooperate on the layers that everybody needs and compete on the customer-facing value that is uniquely yours.

In Web3 this can mean sharing compliance checklists, security practices, or partner maps while competing on product and community. In AI it can mean using common tooling while competing on distribution and trust.

The result is a larger category with clearer differences inside it. That is better for users and better for the teams involved.

Make trust observable

Trust becomes strategy only when it is attached to behavior. Three observable habits matter most:

  • Share roadmaps early enough that a partner can plan around them.
  • Define an escalation path before a deal goes wrong, not after.
  • Give credit publicly where credit is due.

These are small actions. Their cumulative effect is a reputation that lowers the cost of every future deal.

The association perspective

Associations are useful because they create standing relationships. When two organizations have a working history, founders can begin from a known baseline instead of rebuilding credibility from scratch.

This is one reason the Hong Kong Blockchain Association works with partners across regions. Cross-regional Web3 growth needs more than technical bridges. It needs trusted introductions and a shared understanding of what each market expects.

What this means for founders

The practical advice is unglamorous. Attend one event before applying for anything. Arrive with a specific question. Follow up with a short note. Choose partners who will still be useful when the cycle turns.

Markets will keep changing. The founders who get second chances are usually the ones who did not burn the first set of bridges.
